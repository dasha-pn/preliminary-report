The rover employs a parallel-jaw gripper mechanism for maintenance operations and sample collection tasks. The design features a dual-linkage symmetric four-bar configuration maintaining parallel finger pad orientation throughout the \qty{105}{\milli\metre} maximum opening range, ensuring uniform pressure distribution and consistent tool center point (TCP) alignment (\autoref{fig:gripper_assembly}).

\textbf{Mechanical Design and Kinematics}

The symmetric four-bar linkage provides a robust mechanical loop, preventing object rotation and ensuring repeatable force vectors aligned with the TCP. The active gripping surfaces (\numproduct{30 x 32}~mm) feature compliant rubber pads that dampen impact forces and accommodate non-planar geometries. The fingertips narrow to \qty{10}{\milli\metre} for precise engagement with small targets like circuit breakers. The gripper base includes a standardized quick-change mount for rapid attachment to the manipulator. Overall fully open dimensions are \numproduct{180 x 200 x 80}~mm (W$\times$L$\times$H).

\textbf{Actuation and Force Control}

Actuation is driven by a NEMA17 stepper motor, providing sufficient torque and inherent overload protection via step-skipping. Integrated Force-sensitive Hall-effect Dual-Axis (FHDA) load sensors enable closed-loop force control. This allows the system to modulate grip strength based on object compliance, preventing damage to fragile components while ensuring secure retention.

\textbf{Operational Capabilities}

Combined position and force control enables versatile task execution. For maintenance panels, the narrow fingertips and compliant pads allow for pressing buttons with programmable force, rotating knobs through friction coupling, and toggling switches. For sample collection, the \qty{105}{\milli\metre} jaw opening accommodates \qty{30}{\milli\metre} diameter aluminum probes. The parallel mechanism and adaptive grip pressure ensure secure, slip-free retention without structural deformation during extraction and transport.

\textbf{Mechanical Interface}

The gripper base features standardized mounting geometry for attachment to the robotic arm's quick-change end-effector mount, enabling rapid tool exchange. Mechanical and electrical interfaces ensure reliable coupling under field conditions with minimal alignment sensitivity.

\textbf{Astro-Bio Liquid Sampling Attachment}

The liquid sampling attachment is a modular end-effector unit that replaces the standard gripper for aquatic sample collection tasks. It interfaces with the robotic arm's quick-change tool mount, enabling rapid deployment without structural modification to the arm.

The sampling tube is rigid along its length with a flexible distal section to accommodate terrain irregularities during insertion. Liquid intake occurs through a side-facing aperture near the tip to prevent sediment blockage. A hermetically sealed valve at the tube base initiates the intake sequence. Suction is generated by a miniature pump mounted on the arm, drawing fluid through the aperture into a sealed collection container. The container features a top-facing port fitted with a rubber gasket collar, which simultaneously maintains the internal vacuum required for consistent fluid draw and provides a standardized insertion interface for the pH probe without breaking containment (\autoref{fig:sampling_system}).

Sample volume is controlled by a liquid level sensor inside the container, which triggers pump shutdown upon reaching the target fill threshold. A secondary moisture sensor may serve as a pre-trigger to halt tube insertion at appropriate depth. Onboard electronics, including pH and level sensing, are managed by an ESP32 microcontroller; data is relayed via Raspberry Pi and Jetson to the ground station. A display on the ESP32 provides real-time fill-progress feedback during operation.
